# Bluffs and disclosure

What is the value of a bluff after you count lost credibility and revealed information?

<StrategyFigure approach="deception" />

## The decision at the table

In-game deception ranges from keeping your next build private to falsely claiming a shortage or making a promise you do not intend to keep. Those choices can affect immediate offers, later cooperation, and how the table estimates your threat.

## The mechanism to test

Study withholding, selective truth, false factual claims, and promises as separate treatments. Deception requires a claim at odds with the speaker’s known information or stated intent at that time. A mistaken belief or an unforeseen inability to keep a promise is a different event. Keep this vocabulary about gameplay claims.

## Proposed experiment

Begin with truthful versus withholding messages under the same offers. A later, separately registered arm permits one defined bluff class with a fixed frequency cap. Hypothesis: any short-term negotiating gain depends on detectability, recipient skepticism, and how much of the game remains.

Measure immediate response, contradiction opportunities, later trade responses, retaliation, and whole-game wins. Use a credibility timeline rather than assigning an unexplained trust number. Ground truth for message classification comes from the speaker’s own contemporaneous record in a separate diagnostic, never from future knowledge.

## What could disprove it

A bluff that is never challenged is not necessarily believed. The best private payoff may come from a useful trade, not from changing beliefs. Do not generalize performance against gullible scripted bots to humans or skeptical agents.
