# Development and surprise

When does a hidden option justify delaying a visible build?

<StrategyFigure approach="development" />

## The decision at the table

Development cards create possibilities whose value changes with the table: a robber move, a burst of resources, a road opportunity, an army race, or concealed points. Buying a card also postpones other uses of grain and ore.

## The mechanism to test

Compare development purchase and play timing with visible construction. Track publicly known deck depletion without pretending to know other hands. Treat hidden victory potential as a distribution. Consider whether delaying visible points changes targeting, but verify that effect rather than assuming opponents are fooled.

## Proposed experiment

Compare fixed development-card bonuses, deck-aware valuation, and timing-aware valuation with award-race beliefs. Hypothesis: conditional valuation improves wins over indiscriminate purchases while reducing cards that never affect the outcome.

Record buy-to-play time, award transfers, robber impact, and unused development counts at game end. Use public timelines for exhibits. Separate directly observed plays from inferred hidden points and preserve the server’s visibility rules.

## First ablation

Switching the held-card and knight terms off changed the contrast by -0.035 wins per game (95% interval -0.144 to +0.074); raising them by half through the development slider changed it by +0.031 wins per game (95% interval -0.070 to +0.132). Neither is distinguishable from zero. Details and every run ID are in the [ablation report](../../reports/ablations.mdx); this is a development-tier result on one lineup.

## Third round: the timing switches under the learned leaf

The three timing switches were re-run against the learned tables leaf, which buys about twice as many development cards as the hand-written leaf, so card timing could matter more there. Measured as swapped pairs across development, confirmation, and population stages (192 boards per arm), the purchase-tempo rule loses −0.043 wins per game (95% interval −0.076 to −0.009), holding knights for the before-the-roll unblock loses −0.035 (−0.062 to −0.009), and the shaped knight schedule on the quarter blend is worth +0.001 (−0.016 to +0.017). The tables already time purchases and knight plays well, so the hand-tuned rules fight the leaf instead of patching it; every switch stays off. The [timing-tables report](../../reports/timing-tables.mdx) has every pair, the diagnostics, and the browser reading; this is development-tier engine-arena evidence.

## What could disprove it

Largest Army and Longest Road are contests with opponents, not guaranteed point purchases. Spending heavily to take an award briefly may be worse than a permanent build. Evaluate the full continuation.
