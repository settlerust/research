# Expansion and races

When is an expensive road worth buying before an opponent closes the route?

<StrategyFigure approach="expansion" />

## The decision at the table

An attractive empty intersection is not yours until you can reach and settle it. A rival may build across your path, occupy the destination, or win an award race while your cards are committed elsewhere.

## The mechanism to test

Value a road as an option on several future sites. Discount it by the chance the site survives and the cost of defending the route. Keep a fallback plan when the best route is fragile. A sunk road cost is not a reason to continue a losing race.

## Proposed experiment

Compare shortest-path expansion, race-aware expansion, and race-aware expansion with a fallback-site bonus. Freeze production valuation and bargaining. Hypothesis: modelling opponents’ reachable sites reduces stranded roads and improves wins on contested boards.

Retain chosen destination, alternatives, predicted blockage risk, road cost, and whether the destination remained available. Plot route choices on selected public replays and a scatter plot of invested road cards versus points actually reached.

## First ablation

Switching the reachable-site and road-contention terms off changed the contrast by -0.074 wins per game (95% interval -0.169 to +0.021), a small loss whose interval brushes zero; a confirmation on fresh seeds is registered. On 192 fresh seeds the contrast was -0.016 (95% interval -0.078 to +0.047): no effect. Details and every run ID are in the [ablation report](../../reports/ablations.mdx); this is a development-tier result on one lineup.

## Road blocking

Two switches were tested against the unchanged depth-2 search: `opponent_expansion`, which charges the leading opponent's distance-discounted best reachable site and credits roads that raise that distance, and `road_contention_gate`, which restricts the longest-road contention credit to seats leading the table in brick plus lumber production. Measured with the candidate in slot 0 they showed +0.168 and +0.113 wins per game on boards 0 to 63, but those contrasts are the size of the turn-order seating term between two adjacent searches. The registered swapped halves on boards 64 to 127 (control in slot 0) put the seating-corrected effects at +0.008 (−0.053 to +0.069) and −0.004 (−0.061 to +0.053): both consistent with no effect, and the defaults stay off. Breaking an opponent's longest road with a settlement needs no new mechanism; every legal settlement is already a root candidate and the leaf credits the lost award through the opponent-points term.

## What could disprove it

A timid policy can avoid every lost race by surrendering the board. Report missed expansion opportunities too. A counterfactual route is a model prediction; never draw it as an action that occurred.
