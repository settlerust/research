# Ports and market power

How does an outside option change what you should accept in a trade?

<StrategyFigure approach="ports" />

A first, narrower [exchange-aware ETA experiment](../../reports/liquidity-baseline.mdx)
is now complete: 80 valid games, with no established improvement over ETA.
The broader study below remains a proposal.


## The decision at the table

A specialized port can turn surplus into flexibility. But reaching it costs roads and perhaps a weak settlement. Its value falls when the surplus disappears, the robber blocks production, or other players already offer better exchanges.

## The mechanism to test

Compute the best legal bank or port conversion first. That is an outside option against which a player trade can be compared. Then price saved cards, saved time, information revealed, and the opponent’s gain. Market power depends on what others need and can obtain elsewhere, not just how much you hold.

## Proposed experiment

Compare a fixed port bonus with realized-conversion value and a scarcity-aware trade reservation price. Hypothesis: accounting for port acquisition cost and actual surplus avoids overinvestment while preserving profitable specialization.

Measure cards converted, acquisition cost, time until the port is used, bank-versus-player trade share, and wins. Plot cumulative saved cards against the original investment, labelling the resource-accounting convention; saved cards are not victory points.

## First ablation

Switching the port terms off changed the contrast by +0.020 wins per game (95% interval -0.086 to +0.125): no measurable effect at depth 2 against the builders. Details and every run ID are in the [ablation report](../../reports/ablations.mdx); this is a development-tier result on one lineup.

## What could disprove it

Port use alone is not evidence of value. The policy may create surplus by neglecting better builds. Do not infer a monopoly from public production when hidden hands and alternative conversions remain possible.
