---
name: settlers-writing-style
description: Write and edit Settlers research pages, diagram captions, and interface copy in the user's concise, plain style. Use when authoring or revising text for this project.
---

Lead with the finding, decision, or instruction the reader needs. Use familiar
words and connected sentences. Stop when the point is clear.

Do not use em dashes, decorative slogans, rhetorical questions answered by the
next sentence, or sentences that announce what the page will explain. Avoid
stock phrases such as “delve,” “leverage,” “it is worth noting,” and “in short.”
Do not tell readers that an obvious diagram is a diagram or that a visible
control is interactive. Do not fill space with badges, notices, or repeated
disclaimers.

Keep each caption to one short sentence about the relationship or change shown.
For example, write “Surplus grain can fund a road through a port exchange.”
Omit introductions such as “Conceptual diagram showing…” and lists of everything
visible in the figure. Scientific qualifications belong next to the specific
claim they qualify. Identify synthetic data when it could be mistaken for a
measurement; a generic warning on every illustration does not help.

Use a list only for steps, choices, or facts that are easier to compare that way.
Prefer a short paragraph when the ideas form one explanation. Do not turn a
simple answer into several headings, a long inventory, and a repeated conclusion.
Keep useful names, quantities, uncertainty, and evidence references.

Before finishing, remove repetition and sentences that merely state the obvious.
Read the result as a person trying to understand the game, not as an agent
describing its own work. Preserve factual meaning while shortening the text.
