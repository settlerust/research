# Development-card timing under the learned leaf

<ResearchCallout title="Finding · the two timing rules lose under the tables; the knight schedule is worth nothing on a usable blend">
The three development-card switches of the [earlier timing
study](knight-timing.mdx), which corrected to zero under the hand-written
leaf, were re-measured against the learned tables leaf as swapped pairs on
deterministic boards. The tables buy about 4.9 development cards per game
against the hand leaf's 2.6, and the switches that second-guess that appetite
now cost wins. `buy_tempo` (a purchase only when no build is affordable within
the next own turn) reads **−0.043 wins per game** (95% interval −0.076 to
−0.009) over the 192 boards of all three stages, an after-the-fact pooling.
`hold_knight` (a main-phase knight play only when the robber blocks this
seat's best hex) reads **−0.035** (−0.062 to −0.009). `knight_play_value`
(the shaped knight schedule, a leaf term measured on the quarter blend of
tables plus hand-written terms) reads **+0.001** (−0.016 to +0.017). On the
full blend the same switch corrects to **+0.109** (+0.055 to +0.164) on the
development boards, but the full blend itself loses 0.2 wins per game to the
tables alone, so that reading does not transfer. No default changes.
</ResearchCallout>

## Design

Each arm is one switch on the tables baseline seat
(`v2:{"depth":2,"leaf":{"tables":...}}`) measured as a swapped pair: half A
seats the candidate in slot 0 and the control in slot 1, half B swaps them,
both on the same 64 deterministic boards played once per rotation of the four
slots (512 games per pair). The effect is the per-seed half-difference of the
two slot-0-minus-slot-1 contrasts and the seating term their half-sum, which
cancels the arena's turn-order term. Stages, in order: development on boards
0 to 63 with an ETA and a fast builder beside the searches (the lineup of the
hand-leaf cohorts, so the leaves compare on the same boards), confirmation on
fresh boards 800 to 863 in the same lineup, and population on boards 800 to
863 with a third tables search in slot 2 and an ETA in slot 3, so purchases
and knight plays are answered by opponents that trade and block.

`buy_tempo` and `hold_knight` are candidate rules and act under the tables
alone. `knight_play_value` shapes a hand-written leaf term that the tables
alone never evaluate, so its control and candidate both carry the hand-written
terms on top of the tables. A blend screen run in parallel measured the full
blend (hand weight 1.0) at −0.207 wins per game against the tables alone and
the quarter blend at +0.074, so the arm's primary evidence is on the quarter
blend; the development-stage pair on the full blend was already running when
the screen landed and is reported as what it is. One defect had to be fixed
first: the blended leaf dropped the `knight_play_value` flag, which would have
made the switch inert under any blend; the blend now evaluates the hand
terms with the flag, pinned by a test in the server's development suite.

Half B's contrast is slot 0 (control) minus slot 1 (candidate), so a real
effect makes the two halves point in opposite directions. Both `buy_tempo`
halves point the same way on the development and confirmation boards: the
candidate loses more in slot 1 than the seating term explains, and the
corrected sign is negative in all three stages. The seating terms themselves
(+0.08 to +0.14 on L1, near zero on L2 with a third search seated between)
match the earlier null pairs on these boards.

## What the switches did

mechanisms all fire; the first two simply fire against the tables' own
timing.

Under the hand-written leaf the tempo rule halved purchases (1.54 against
2.55 per game) and advanced the first city by a turn without winning. Under
the tables it cuts only a fifth of the purchases (about 3.2 against 3.9 to
4.2), still builds the extra cities (1.4 against 1.2), and now loses. The
tables already spend on cards when the board justifies it, so a rigid "no
build within a turn" gate mostly removes good buys. Holding knights forgoes
about 0.2 plays and a fifth of the army awards per game under the tables,
half the bite it had under the hand leaf, and loses more at the population
table, where a rival search punishes the held robber. The knight schedule
changes almost no count on the quarter blend: the tables' own knight
valuation leaves the shaped schedule nothing to correct, which is why its
intervals are the tightest of the three arms. On the full blend the same
schedule is worth +0.109, which reads as the hand-written flat knight weight
being wrong at full strength, not as a gain available to any configuration
worth running.

Decision cost moves only where the candidate list changes: `buy_tempo` prunes
the purchase branch and decides about 30% faster than the control, while
`hold_knight` and `knight_play_value` cost the same as their controls. The
blends cost about 900 to 1000 ms per decision at depth 2 under load, against
450 to 750 ms for the tables alone and about 200 to 250 ms for the
hand-written leaf, because the hand terms are evaluated at every leaf on top
of the tables.

## Comparison with the hand-leaf results

Under the hand-written leaf (the earlier study, same development boards) the
single-seating contrasts were +0.043 (`buy_tempo`, boards 0-63) and +0.098
and +0.066 (`hold_knight`), all inside the seating term, and the
`knight_play_value` swapped pair corrected to +0.004 (−0.061 to +0.069). The
sign pattern flips under the tables: what was harmless under a leaf that
under-bought cards is harmful under a leaf that buys them well. The tables
learned card timing from self-play, and the hand-written timing rules, tuned
by intuition, now fight the leaf instead of patching it.

## What could still be wrong

All evidence here is engine-arena development tier at depth 2 on deterministic
boards, two lineups, and one frozen table file; nothing is protocol-tier.
The population lineup still seats an ETA builder, so "opponents that trade
and block" means one more search, not three humans. The `buy_tempo` estimate
mixes a refuted development stage with two inconclusive ones; the pooled
interval excludes zero but pooling across stages is after the fact. The
quarter blend itself is a screen reading (+0.074, interval +0.011 to +0.138,
unregistered) chosen by a parallel study; if a different blend becomes the
default, the `knight_play_value` reading scales with the hand weight and
should be re-read at that weight. The full-blend pair is one stage on one
lineup, so +0.109 is the thinnest reading here. Decision times come from a
machine shared at high load and compare candidate with control within each
run, which cancels the load but not the noise.

## What this means for the browser

The browser build runs the hand-written leaf at depth 3 under a one-second
budget, and none of the three switches earns a place there. The bar is a
pooled effect of at least +0.03 wins per game with the interval excluding
zero and decision time within 25% of the control's; no arm comes close on
either leaf. `buy_tempo` was zero under the hand leaf and loses under the
tables (−0.043 pooled). It does decide about 30% faster than its control in
both leaves, but a cheaper search is only useful when the strength holds:
here the strength is absent under the hand leaf and negative under the
tables, so the saving buys nothing. `hold_knight` loses under the tables
(−0.035 pooled) and showed nothing under the hand leaf. `knight_play_value`
is zero under the hand leaf and under the quarter blend; its only positive
reading sits on the full blend, a configuration too weak to ship. All three
switches stay off by default, in the engine and in the browser.
