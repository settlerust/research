## The question

The frozen tables (`hex-portfolio-main`, the leaf of `ntuple-leaf`) were trained
by self-play among four copies of themselves, guarded only by two builders at
validation time. A player trained that way may be strong against its own style
and weak against a player that has seen it. Two questions, in order. How much
does a challenger gain when it is trained specifically against the frozen
tables, and does that gain survive a table of builders? And do tables trained
on a mixed population beat the frozen tables at both lineups while giving up
less to the challenger?

## The arms

All three arms start from the frozen tables (`train --init`) and learn with
the same rule (TD(lambda) at depth 1 on the true state, alpha 0.5, chunks of
5,000 games, a validation tournament of 48 boards against the hand-written
leaf with the builders after every chunk, the best validation point frozen).
They differ only in who sits at the training table, drawn by weight for every
game with `train --pool` (a draw without the learner is redrawn; only the
learner's seats update the tables).

## The evidence

Every contrast is a swapped pair on the same deterministic boards: the
candidate in slot 0 and the control in slot 1, then the other way round; the
effect is half the per-board difference of the two slot-0-minus-slot-1
contrasts, which cancels the turn-order [seating term](arena-seating.mdx).
The builder table (L1) seats ETA and fast in slots 2 and 3. The population
table (L2) seats a third frozen-tables search and ETA, so the candidate meets
two frozen seats. Exploitability was measured on boards 1400 to 1463, the
remedy on boards 1464 to 1527; all boards were fresh. Every cohort is 64
boards, four rotations, 256 deterministic games.
