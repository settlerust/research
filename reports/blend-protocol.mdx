# The quarter blend through the protocol arena

<ResearchCallout title="Inconclusive: the blend and the tables alone split the protocol pair">
Seated inside `ntuple-leaf`'s exact search, the quarter-strength blend won <WinRate value="34" total="80" label="blend, half A" /> and <WinRate value="34" total="80" label="blend, half B" /> against `ntuple-leaf`'s <WinRate value="33" total="80" label="half A" /> and <WinRate value="32" total="80" label="half B" />. The seating-corrected effect is <ResultComparison value="0.019" low="-0.087" high="0.124" />, below the +0.03 bar with an interval that covers both zero and the engine estimate of +0.066. `ntuple-leaf` stays the protocol reference.
</ResearchCallout>

## What was registered

The blend is `ntuple-leaf`'s search (depth 3, 1.5 s budget, 8 dice scenarios,
4 sampled worlds, 4 inner scenarios) with `leaf.hand` 0.25 added on top of
the same frozen tables, registered in the policy registry as
`ntuple-leaf-blend`. The [engine retest](tables-retest.mdx) had read it at
+0.066 wins per game against builders and +0.064 at a table of searches, at
1.4 times the decision time, and the [current evidence](../docs/status.md)
asked for a protocol cohort before any default changes.

Two halves were registered under `patterns/blend-protocol` before either
ran, each naming the other:
[half A](https://github.com/settlerust/research/blob/dev/experiments/0d174884-6e7e-4d88-8f1c-dd51cb21ba29.toml)
seats the blend in slot 0 and `ntuple-leaf` in slot 1,
[half B](https://github.com/settlerust/research/blob/dev/experiments/23b1085d-20a9-473f-a31d-daa907181449.toml)
swaps them; `eta` and `fast` fill slots 2 and 3 in both. Each half plays
deterministic seeds 2000 to 2019 once per rotation of the four slots, 80
games, with the same points, match, decision, and turn caps as the
[cohort that made `ntuple-leaf` the baseline](ntuple-leaf-protocol.mdx).
The rule: all 160 games valid with no server timeout moves; support if the
seating-corrected effect's interval lies above zero and the estimate reaches
+0.03 wins per game; refutation if the interval lies below zero; otherwise
inconclusive.

## The seating-corrected effect

| Contrast | Wins per game (95% interval over 20 seeds) |
| --- | --- |
| Half A, slot 0 minus slot 1 (blend minus `ntuple-leaf`) | +0.013 (−0.156 to +0.181) |
| Half B, slot 0 minus slot 1 (`ntuple-leaf` minus blend) | −0.025 (−0.195 to +0.145) |
| Seating-corrected effect of the blend | **+0.019 (−0.087 to +0.124)** |
| Seating term | −0.006 (−0.139 to +0.126) |

The blend won one more game than the tables alone in half A and two more in
half B, and the two seatings agree that there is nothing large to find: the
corrected estimate is +0.019 wins per game, below the +0.03 bar, with an
interval that includes zero, so the registered rule reads inconclusive. It
is not a refutation. Twenty boards played in both seatings resolve about
±0.11 wins per game, and the engine estimate of +0.066 sits comfortably
inside the interval; this cohort could not separate an effect of that size
from nothing. The seating term is invisible here (−0.006), unlike the engine
arena's +0.05 to +0.18, which says nothing about the arena difference on
twenty boards.

What the blend changes at the table is the same as in the engine: over both
halves it built more cities per game (1.43 and 1.38 against 1.09 and 1.14),
traded more with players (2.30 and 2.46 against 1.96 and 2.14), and finished
with about half a point more (7.79 and 7.81 against 7.33 and 7.24), and
those points did not become wins.

## Decision time and completed depth

The `remote` runner's search seat now writes an optional per-decision trace
(`SETTLERS_SEARCH_TRACE`, kept in each seat's private policy log), so this
is the first protocol cohort that can say how much of the depth-3 search the
1.5 s clock actually delivered. The machine was shared with five other
agents throughout (load average 60 to 80 on 32 cores), and both seats in
every game shared that load.

| Half | Policy | Contested decisions | Mean ms | Median ms | Completed depth 3 | Depth 2 | Depth 1 | Stopped by the budget |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A | `ntuple-leaf-blend` | 3,267 | 932 | 882 | 49.9% | 42.8% | 7.3% | 26.0% |
| A | `ntuple-leaf` | 3,073 | 827 | 732 | 61.2% | 35.3% | 3.5% | 20.2% |
| B | `ntuple-leaf` | 3,267 | 834 | 749 | 60.9% | 35.4% | 3.7% | 21.8% |
| B | `ntuple-leaf-blend` | 3,422 | 911 | 853 | 51.8% | 42.1% | 6.2% | 25.3% |

Contested decisions are main-phase decisions with at least two root actions;
a single candidate never deepens past the first depth. Over all decisions,
including the trivial ones, the blend's median was 516 and 478 ms against
427 and 456 ms. The blend spent about 1.1 times the control's time per
contested decision, less than the engine's 1.4 because the clock caps the
difference, and paid for it in depth: it completed depth 3 in half of its
contested decisions where the tables alone completed it in three fifths, and
it was stopped by the budget in a quarter of them against a fifth. Both
seats fell back to the first legal action on a search error in about 0.2%
of decisions (22 and 19 for the blend, 19 and 27 for the tables alone, out
of 9,000 to 10,000 decisions per seat per half), a pre-existing path of the
search when it returns no candidate.

## Limitations

One lineup, one rotation scheme, twenty boards: a swapped pair on twenty
seeds resolves about ±0.11 wins per game, so only a large effect could clear
the +0.03 bar here, and the engine estimate of +0.066 sits inside what this
cohort cannot separate from zero. The two searches are time-budgeted, and
the cohort ran on a machine shared with five other agents, so both seats
completed depth 3 less often than the unloaded 2026-09-09 cohort would have,
and the blend lost about ten points of depth-3 share to its cost. The
contrast is fair within a game, but it measures the two evaluators as the
machine could run them, not at full depth. A first attempt at half A stopped
on a seat-join conflict at game creation
([abandoned attempt](/research/records/runs/99bfec6f-10ac-4536-99bf-460d62d0fea5),
18 valid games, not pooled) and the harness's join step was made to retry on
conflict before the pair was played; the [daily log](../log/2026-09-25.mdx)
has the details. Nothing here speaks to negotiating opponents, humans, or
the browser's hand-written leaf.

## What the browser would need

The browser worker runs the hand-written leaf in WASM and cannot load the
tables, so `leaf.hand` is not a browser switch. Using the blend there would
need the 14.3 MB tables shipped to the worker, which the WASM build rejects
by decision, or a server-side seat; nothing in web changes with this result.
