# What each mechanism is worth

<ResearchCallout title="Finding · two levers dominate, the rest are small">
On 64 deterministic boards, each played once per seat rotation, removing the
opponents' round and the next turn (depth 1) cost **−0.324 wins per game**
(95% interval −0.413 to −0.235) against an unchanged depth-2 control, and
averaging the round over two dice scenarios instead of eight cost **−0.410**
(−0.489 to −0.331). Every other single change, whether a leaf term switched
off or a style slider pushed to its limit, moved the contrast by less than
0.1 wins per game with an interval that crossed zero.
</ResearchCallout>

## How the arms were built

Every arm seats a search with exactly one change in slot 0 and the unchanged
depth-2 control in slot 1, with an ETA builder and a fast builder in the other
seats. Games run in deterministic mode, so both searches and both builders face
the same board, the same k-th roll, the same k-th theft, and the same deck
order on every seed; each seed is played once per rotation of the four slots.
The primary outcome is the per-seed difference in wins between slots 0 and 1
with a normal-approximation interval over 64 seeds. Depth 2 is the control
because it is representative and four times cheaper than the confirmed
depth-3 candidate; a change that helps at depth 2 is a candidate for the deep
configuration, not a proof.

## The arms

| Approach | Change tested | Treatment wins | Control wins | Contrast (95% interval) |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Search | depth 1: no lookahead | 50 | 133 | −0.324 (−0.413 to −0.235) |
| Search | two dice scenarios instead of eight | 27 | 132 | −0.410 (−0.489 to −0.331) |
| Beliefs | hands sampled from counts only, no event history | 78 | 101 | −0.090 (−0.201 to +0.021) |
| Beliefs | one sampled world instead of four | 89 | 102 | −0.051 (−0.166 to +0.064) |
| Bargaining | no trade margin (trade slider at 1) | 80 | 104 | −0.094 (−0.192 to +0.005) |
| Bargaining | never propose a trade | 83 | 90 | −0.027 (−0.132 to +0.077) |
| Expansion | site-value and road-contention terms off | 87 | 106 | −0.074 (−0.169 to +0.021) |
| Risk | risk slider at 1 (half the discard weight) | 88 | 104 | −0.062 (−0.175 to +0.050) |
| Risk | discard-exposure term off | 90 | 96 | −0.023 (−0.134 to +0.087) |
| Opening | builder placement instead of search placement | 87 | 96 | −0.035 (−0.133 to +0.062) |
| Development | card and knight terms off | 91 | 100 | −0.035 (−0.144 to +0.074) |
| Development | development slider at 1 | 98 | 90 | +0.031 (−0.070 to +0.132) |
| Adaptation | leaf-guided opponents instead of the builder model | 88 | 90 | −0.008 (−0.111 to +0.096) |
| Containment | all opponent terms off | 96 | 96 | 0.000 (−0.105 to +0.105) |
| Containment | aggression slider at 1 | 99 | 94 | +0.020 (−0.084 to +0.123) |
| Ports | port terms off | 97 | 92 | +0.020 (−0.086 to +0.125) |
| Tempo | acquisition-time plan term off | 105 | 89 | +0.062 (−0.039 to +0.164) |

Each row is 256 games. Removing the plan term did not hurt at depth 2, which
was unexpected: the card, production, and hand terms already carry most of the
acquisition-time signal once the search plans a whole turn, so the term that
rescued the first leaf is now nearly redundant. Six arms whose intervals
brushed zero (event knowledge, trade margin, expansion terms, risk slider,
sampled worlds, and the plan term) are registered again on 192 fresh seeds;
their results are added below when they complete.

## Four styles at one table

<ResearchChart asset="ablation-crossplay-wins" />

Four depth-2 searches that differ only in their inclination presets (builder,
raider, gambler, trader) split a table almost evenly: 66, 59, 69, and 62 wins
of 256. The sliders change what a seat builds and how it trades without
changing how often it wins against equals, which is what a style control
should do.

## What this program cannot test

Truthful signals, bluffs, and bounded pliability need messages and a
recipient that reads them; the fixed builders ignore conversation. Reputation
has one mechanical proxy, a willingness model that learns each partner's
acceptance rate from the public trade record, and its arm is running.
