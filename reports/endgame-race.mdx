# Endgame racing and stopping the leader

<ResearchCallout title="Finding · three endgame switches win on development seeds, none is confirmed">
The search treats a seat at 8 points like one at 4 except through two linear
weights. Three switches, each tested alone, changed that. A race leaf that
replaces the opponent terms with the own-minus-leader margin plus the leader's
expected points next turn won its development cohort by **+0.129 wins per
game** (95% interval +0.029 to +0.228), counting expected hidden victory points
by **+0.172** (+0.080 to +0.264), and a late robber and road bias toward the
leader by **+0.168** (+0.073 to +0.263), all on 256 deterministic games on seeds
0 to 63. On fresh seeds 64 to 127 the same three arms fell to **+0.078**
(−0.010 to +0.166), **+0.039** (−0.049 to +0.127), and **+0.051** (−0.044 to
+0.146): every direction held, every confirmation interval crossed zero. No
switch is confirmed and the defaults stay unchanged.
</ResearchCallout>

## The three switches

All three sit behind one `endgame` object in the seat configuration, off by
default, so `v2:{"depth":2}` is unchanged. Each was tested with exactly one
switch on, the unchanged depth-2 control in slot 1, and an ETA and a fast
builder in the other seats.

The **race leaf** (`endgame.race`) arms once any opponent is within
`endgame.trigger` points, two by default, of victory. It drops the plain
`opponent_progress` and `opponent_production` terms and adds two: the
own-minus-leader margin, and the leader's expected new points next turn,
estimated as the best of a city, a reachable settlement, and a development
purchase, each times the share of its cost their hand plus one roll of their
production covers, with the purchase priced at the deck's remaining
victory-card share. Blocked production counts for less, so moving the robber
onto the leader's best hex also slows the estimate.

The **hidden points** switch (`endgame.hidden_points`) keeps every term and
only changes what counts as an opponent's score: the victory cards sampled into
each hidden world from the remaining deck prior, on top of public points. A
leaf averaged over sampled worlds therefore counts expected hidden victory
points inferred from public purchases and plays, without seeing any private
hand.

The **leader bias** (`endgame.block_leader`) touches only the candidate
shortlists, never the leaf, and only after two thirds of the estimated game
length. Robber placements score the leader's blocked production and stolen
cards with extra weight, and a road that lengthens the leader's route to the
best site both seats contest scores part of that site's value, a road the plain
reach score never pays for.

## The cohorts

Each row is one registered cohort, 64 deterministic seeds played once per
four-seat rotation, 256 games, the same lineup for treatment and control.

| Switch | Seeds | Treatment wins | Control wins | Contrast (95% interval) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Race leaf | 0 to 63 | 121 | 88 | +0.129 (+0.029 to +0.228) |
| Hidden points | 0 to 63 | 129 | 85 | +0.172 (+0.080 to +0.264) |
| Leader bias | 0 to 63 | 127 | 84 | +0.168 (+0.073 to +0.263) |
| Race leaf | 64 to 127 | 110 | 90 | +0.078 (−0.010 to +0.166) |
| Hidden points | 64 to 127 | 107 | 97 | +0.039 (−0.049 to +0.127) |
| Leader bias | 64 to 127 | 108 | 95 | +0.051 (−0.044 to +0.146) |

The three development cohorts met their registered rules: the interval excluded
zero and the mean reached +0.05 wins per game. The confirmations did not. Each
direction stayed positive on fresh boards but with an interval that crosses
zero, so each is inconclusive, not a refutation. Unregistered 16-seed screens
on seeds 0 to 15 pointed the same way (+0.16, +0.25, +0.30 raw win
differences) and carry no evidential weight.

## Diagnostics

The registered diagnostics come from the per-game records: games the treatment
seat lost by exactly one point, how often the final-points leader was robbed
(a game-level count over all four seats), and the final point gap.

| Switch | Cohort | Lost by exactly one point | Leader robbed per game | Share of robberies on the leader | Winner minus treatment points |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Race leaf | 0 to 63 | 16 of 135 losses | 4.18 | 0.297 | 1.77 |
| Hidden points | 0 to 63 | 14 of 127 losses | 4.09 | 0.294 | 1.79 |
| Leader bias | 0 to 63 | 11 of 129 losses | 4.25 | 0.299 | 1.88 |
| Race leaf | 64 to 127 | 7 of 146 losses | 4.47 | 0.309 | 2.28 |
| Hidden points | 64 to 127 | 6 of 149 losses | 4.56 | 0.312 | 2.37 |
| Leader bias | 64 to 127 | 5 of 148 losses | 4.40 | 0.305 | 2.41 |

The leader-bias switch did not measurably move the game-level robber
statistics: about three robberies in ten land on the final-points leader
across all six cohorts, whether or not any seat runs the bias. The diagnostics
count every seat's robberies, so a one-seat switch is diluted by three seats
that rob by their own lights, and the final-points leader is only a proxy for
the seat that looked dangerous at the time. The one-point losses are the same
story from the other side: the treatment seats lost fewer one-point games in
the confirmations, but the counts are small.

## What this says and what it does not

Against two fixed builders and one identical search, all three mechanisms
point the same way on the first 64 boards and shrink on the next 64. The
pooled reading is that each switch is worth somewhere between nothing and
about a tenth of a win per game, which 256 games cannot separate from zero.
This is engine-arena evidence on one lineup; the opponents never retaliate,
negotiate, or adjust, so a bias that the builders cannot punish may be easier
to carry in this arena than at a real table. Nothing here tests the switches
together, at the deeper confirmed configurations, or against the learned
tables leaf, whose endgame value already includes opponent scores.

The switches stay off by default and are ready for a follow-up: a combined
arm, stronger race weights, or the race leaf under the depth-3 and
`ntuple-leaf` configurations, registered on fresh seed ranges before any
default change.
