## What one second buys

<ResearchPlot asset="search-rate-wasm" />

The browser worker asks for depth 3 under a one-second clock, so the question
behind every arm is how many engine transitions that second buys. The
structure tool sampled 70 main-phase positions from builder games on seeds 100
to 103 (every ninth decision after setup with more than one legal action,
seats and turns mixed), each carrying the seat's own event history. The
current WASM package, rebuilt from the server's `dev` branch and identical
byte for byte to the one the web checkout carries, analysed every position in
Node at depth 2 and depth 3 with iterative deepening off, no clock, and a node
budget that never binds, three times each with the fastest repeat kept. A
depth-2 analysis is 16,976 transitions at the median position (9,189 at the
25th percentile, 31,890 at the 75th, 71,404 at the 90th); a depth-3 analysis
is 65,677 (35,907, 122,926, and 235,562), so the cost of the third planned
turn varies by a factor of seven across the middle 80% of positions.

The machine carried a load average between 88 and 160 on 32 logical CPUs
from other agents' tournaments throughout, which decides how the rate must be
read. By wall clock the search ran 41.9 thousand transitions per second at the
median depth-3 position (28.1 thousand at the 25th percentile), and the native
release binary measured on the same positions under the same load ran 45.9
thousand: a ratio of 1.03 where the idle measurement of 2026-09-09 found 1.38,
so the wall clock was measuring contention, not the runtime. By CPU time,
which counts only the moments the worker's thread was scheduled, the rate is
82.8 thousand transitions per second at the median position and 64.5 thousand
at the 25th percentile, at depth 3; depth 2 runs about a fifth faster per
transition because its trees stay in cache. An idle worker on this processor
would run faster still, since a fully loaded chip shares each core between two
threads and drops its clock; the 2026-09-09 idle timings put a depth-3
analysis at 312 ms on positions of similar cost, roughly twice the CPU rate
measured here. A laptop browser sits somewhere between the two.

The budget for the cohorts is therefore **N = 83,000 transitions**, one second
of CPU at the median position, with 65,000 as the 25th-percentile reading. At
N the depth-3 pass fits in 61% of the sampled positions and the depth-2 pass in
96%; at the 25th-percentile budget the depth-3 pass fits in 49%.

## The arms

Every arm is a swapped pair on the same deterministic boards with an ETA and a
fast builder in the other two seats: half A seats the candidate in slot 0 and
the control in slot 1, half B the other way round, and the effect is half the
per-seed difference of the two slot-0-minus-slot-1 contrasts, which cancels the
[turn-order seating term](arena-seating.mdx) exactly. The control is the
browser's search with the clock replaced by the budget:
`v2:{"depth":3,"time_budget_ms":0,"node_budget":83000}`, the hand-written
leaf, four sampled worlds, eight scenarios, four inner scenarios, iterative
deepening. With the clock off the node budget bounds each depth pass, so a
depth-3 pass that needs more than N transitions is abandoned and the depth-2
answer is played, which is what the browser does when its second runs out.
Each candidate changes one setting of that control. The two deepening-share
arms need a budget that all passes of a decision share, since the 30% rule
reads how much of the budget the earlier passes used; a new `total_budget`
switch in the search counts the node budget the way the clock is counted, and
both seats of those two arms carry it, so their control differs from the
others' by that switch alone.

Games are deterministic, so the same budget binds the same way for both seats
whatever the machine is doing, and wall-clock decision times below are only
comparable within a pair: the machine carried a load average between 120 and
170 throughout, three to five times the idle cost.

### Screens on 16 boards

Eleven arms were more than the machine could carry through two stages of 512
games each while nine other agents ran their own cohorts, so every arm was
first screened as an unregistered swapped pair on boards 0 to 15 (64 games per
half, 128 per arm). A screen resolves about ±0.15 wins per game and is not a
finding; it decides which arms go on to the registered stages. Depth-3
completion and abort shares are over every search the seat answered, including
the roll-phase and single-candidate positions that stop at depth 1 by rule;
the four setup placements per game are decided by the opening planner and count
as depth 0.

| Arm | Candidate wins | Control wins | Screen effect (95% interval) | Seating term | Depth 3 completed | Aborted | Decision time |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| depth 2 | 47 | 58 | −0.086 (−0.230 to +0.059) | +0.117 | 0.0% vs 48.4% | 0.7% vs 13.3% | 400 vs 1354 ms |
| cache decision | 62 | 51 | +0.086 (−0.062 to +0.234) | +0.102 | 55.4% vs 49.1% | 6.6% vs 13.4% | 1597 vs 1922 ms |
| samples 2 | 58 | 48 | +0.078 (−0.011 to +0.167) | +0.062 | 59.3% vs 49.6% | 2.7% vs 12.4% | 1096 vs 1674 ms |
| inner scenarios 2 | 55 | 52 | +0.023 (−0.080 to +0.126) | +0.055 | 57.3% vs 49.1% | 5.1% vs 12.6% | 1215 vs 1541 ms |
| scenarios 4 | 18 | 79 | −0.477 (−0.624 to −0.330) | −0.039 | 61.0% vs 49.9% | 2.7% vs 13.2% | 830 vs 1492 ms |
| deepen 1.0 (total budget) | 54 | 52 | +0.016 (−0.047 to +0.078) | +0.109 | 42.4% vs 41.6% | 19.9% vs 5.8% | 1191 vs 921 ms |
| deepen 0.5 (total budget) | 54 | 52 | +0.016 (−0.047 to +0.078) | +0.109 | 42.4% vs 41.6% | 13.7% vs 5.8% | 1214 vs 1005 ms |

Three things are already clear at this resolution. Halving the common random
scenarios is ruinous: the seat with four scenarios won 18 games to the
control's 79, an effect of −0.477 whose interval sits far from zero, matching
the −0.410 that two scenarios cost at depth 2 in the 2026-09-09 ablations, and
the depth-3 completions it buys (61% against 50%) do not come close to paying
for the noisier opponents' round. Under a budget shared by every pass, letting
the search attempt depth 3 whenever budget remains changes almost nothing:
attempts rise (aborts 20% against 6%) but completions barely move (42.4%
against 41.6%), because a third pass that starts with less than 70% of the
budget almost never finishes, so the 0.5 and 1.0 arms played the same games
as each other. The two arms that cut the cost of a pass, two sampled worlds and
tables shared across passes, each raised the depth-3 completion share by six to
ten points, cut aborts by half or more, and screened positive; they went on to
the registered stages with the depth-2 question, and the inner-scenario arm
followed as a weaker version of the same mechanism.
